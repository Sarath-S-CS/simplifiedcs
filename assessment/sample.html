Access & Identity · Is multi-factor authentication enforced for remote and admin access? - your answer: "Admin accounts only"
Critical gap Effort: Low Target: 30 days
▸
  • Why it's critical: MFA covers admin accounts only, and you have internet-reachable services (cloud or remote access), so ordinary accounts can be taken over with a password alone.
  • Why it ranks here: Your answer: "Admin accounts only" · Critical control (CISA Performance Goals / #StopRansomware priority) · MFA covers admin accounts only, and you have internet-reachable services (cloud or remote access), so ordinary accounts can be taken over with a password alone.
  • What could go wrong: MFA is the specific control that stops a leaked or guessed password alone from being enough to log in as a legitimate user.
  • Attack technique: MITRE ATT&CK T1078 - Valid Accounts
  • Interim step: Prioritize MFA on admin and remote-access accounts first - that's the smallest rollout that closes the largest share of risk.
  • Durable fix: Enforce MFA for every account, not just admins - most identity providers (Microsoft Entra, Google Workspace, Okta) support org-wide enforcement from one setting.
  • Suggested owner: IT lead
  • Evidence it's done: Identity-provider report showing MFA enforced for all users and admin roles
  • Framework references: NIST CSF 2.0: PR.AA-03 · CIS Controls v8.1: 6.3, 6.4, 6.5

Critical control Effort: Medium Target: 30 days
▸
  • Why it ranks here: Your answer: "Scheduled but inconsistent" · Critical control (CISA Performance Goals / #StopRansomware priority)
  • What could go wrong: Unpatched software is what turns an already-known, already-fixed vulnerability into a working exploit - attackers routinely scan for exactly this rather than looking for something novel.

  • Suggested owner: IT lead
  • Evidence it's done: Patch-compliance report and the written patching timeframes
  • Framework references: NIST CSF 2.0: PR.PS-02 · CIS Controls v8.1: 7.3, 7.4, 7.7

Access & Identity · How are privileged accounts (domain admins, database admins, local/server administrators) managed? - your answer: "Individually assigned, but not regularly reviewed"
Critical control Effort: Medium Target: 30 days
▸
  • Why it ranks here: Your answer: "Individually assigned, but not regularly reviewed" · Critical control (CISA Performance Goals / #StopRansomware priority)
  • What could go wrong: Shared or long-standing privileged credentials are a high-value target precisely because they're rarely rotated and often outlive the reason they were created - one compromise can go unnoticed for a long time.
  • Attack technique: MITRE ATT&CK T1078 - Valid Accounts
  • Interim step: As an interim step, do a one-time manual review of who currently holds privileged access and revoke anything clearly stale - a single pass now is better than waiting for a full recurring review process.
  • Durable fix: Individually assign every privileged account (no shared logins), and schedule a recurring (at least quarterly) access review with least-privilege enforcement.
  • Suggested owner: IT lead
  • Evidence it's done: Privileged account inventory with owners and last review/rotation dates
  • Framework references: NIST CSF 2.0: PR.AA-01, PR.AA-05 · CIS Controls v8.1: 5.1, 5.2, 5.4

Recovery & Continuity · How often are backups tested by actually restoring data? - your answer: "Rarely"
Critical control Effort: Low Target: 30 days
▸
  • Why it ranks here: Your answer: "Rarely" · Critical control (CISA Performance Goals / #StopRansomware priority)
  • What could go wrong: This is the ransomware technique - and an untested backup is a backup whose recoverability is simply unknown until the day it matters most.
  • Attack technique: MITRE ATT&CK T1486 - Data Encrypted for Impact
  • Interim step: Backups are at least isolated from the production network, which is the part that matters most against ransomware specifically - test-restore a single file or system now for a first real data point.
  • Durable fix: Schedule quarterly (at minimum) backup-restore tests against real systems, documenting actual restore time against your required recovery targets.
  • Suggested owner: IT lead
  • Evidence it's done: Restore test log with dates, systems restored and time taken
  • Framework references: NIST CSF 2.0: PR.DS-11, RC.RP-03 · CIS Controls v8.1: 11.5
  • Reference: CISA: StopRansomware

High impact Effort: Low Target: 60 days
▸
